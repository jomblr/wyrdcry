# Design Principles
These principles have been designed to allow other

### 1. Warcry is the vessel
Wyrdcry runs on the fast-paced skirmish engine that is Warcry. That part is non-negotiable, as the speed and simplicity of Warcry is what got this project started in the first place. That means  alternating activations, fluid movement and quick-to-resolve attack actions.

The game is not a perfect simulation and requires players to spin their own story around the dice rolls. It does not seek to portray every blow, dodge and parry.

Complexity is welcome when it creates an interesting decision, like when building your warband and deciding what to equip your fighters with. We do not need to precisely determine where a falling fighter crashes to the ground or if a high Defense characteristic stems from being a great duelist or being decked out in heavy armour.

---

### 2. Mordheim is the soul
Wyrdcry is an alternate ruleset for playing Mordheim campaigns. Players may adapt the ruleset for other settings, but at the end of the day, the feel of the hack should be that of the grimdark, Warhammer Fantasy city that chews people up, where fish fall from the sky and the rise of Morrslieb makes the Wyrdstone shriek in an eerie howl.

This means that we must respect the setting and the lore when writing rules and designing the mechanical identity of the factions. If a rule feels mechanically right but wrong for the lore of the faction, the lore wins.

---

### 3. Battles worth fighting
The goal for the hack has been to create memorable battles that you want to stick around for, not feeding your opponent henchmen until you can voluntarily rout and roll dice in an aftermath sequence.

A Wyrdcry battle should feel chaotic and swingy, it should reward a good strategist but also embrace moments where a plan falls apart. If you’re outnumbered and under-equipped, you should still be rewarded for engaging in battle - it’s not something dreadful you should have to suffer through to collect some arbitrary benefit afterwards.

---

### 4. Death is real and meaningful
Wyrdcry is designed for campaign play, which means that players will be (and should be!) worried about losing fighters in a way that you rarely see in one-off skirmish games. Players will not always make risky plays that puts their veterans lives at risk, but when they do, it should feel exciting and meaningful.

The ruleset should bite hard enough that it hurts whenever a fighter is taken out of action, but not so hard that players feel punished for fighting the battle (see #3). This must also be considered during the aftermath sequence, where a warband losing multiple fighters will start a downward death-spiral that is hard to recover from.

---

### 5. Easy to pick up
Wyrdcry is a fan-made hack, and should not require players to heavily invest into new miniatures or terrain. Players should be able to use their original Mordheim warbands to get started, and be allowed to use whatever models or base sizes they want. Wyrdcry is model-agnostic.

When the playtests are over and we launch the 1.0 version, the rules should be easy to access, and errata and balance updates minimal. This will never be a competitive game and does not have to be perfectly balanced, and players shouldn’t have to re-learn the game with monthly or quarterly updates. However, the original 6 warbands should be balanced enough so that they can be used as a measuring stick when developing other warbands.
