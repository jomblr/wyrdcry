# Design Principles

### 1. Warcry is the vessel
The speed and simplicity of Warcry is what got this project started in the first place, so honoring that kind of fluid gameplay (alternating activations, fluid movement, simple attack actions) is non-negotiable. 

We do not aim to build a perfect simulation and trust the players to spin their own story around the dice rolls. There's no need to portray every blow, dodge or parry; and we do not need to precisely determine where a falling fighter crashes to the ground.

It is okay to add complexity when it creates an interesting decision, like building your warband or when deciding what to equip your fighters with, or how to position your fighters to get better modifiers.

---

### 2. Mordheim is the soul
Wyrdcry is an alternate ruleset for playing Mordheim campaigns. It is set during the war of the three emperors, in the former Ostermark capital, where fish fall from the sky and the rise of Morrslieb makes the wyrdstone shriek in an eerie howl.

We respect the setting when writing rules and design the mechanical identity of the factions. If a rule or warband feels mechanically right, but out of place for the lore, the lore wins.

---

### 3. Battles worth fighting
The goal for the hack has been to create memorable battles that you want to stick around for, not feeding your opponent henchmen until you can voluntarily rout and roll dice in an aftermath sequence.

A Wyrdcry battle should feel chaotic and swingy, it should reward a good strategist but also embrace moments where a plan falls apart. If you’re outnumbered and under-equipped, you should still be rewarded for engaging in battle - it’s not something dreadful you should have to suffer through to collect some arbitrary benefit afterwards.

---

### 4. Death is real and meaningful
Wyrdcry is designed for campaign play, which means that players will be (and should be!) worried about losing fighters. Players will not always make risky plays that puts the lives of their heroes at risk, but when they do, it should feel exciting and meaningful.

You should worry whenever a fighter is taken out of action, but not so hard that you feel punished for fighting the battle (see #3). This must also be considered during the aftermath sequence, where a warband losing multiple fighters will start a downward death-spiral that is hard to recover from.

---

### 5. Easy to pick up
Wyrdcry is a fan-made hack, and should not require players to heavily invest into new miniatures or terrain. Players should be able to use their original Mordheim warbands to get started, and be allowed to use whatever models or base sizes they want. Wyrdcry is model-agnostic.

The rules should be easy to access, and errata and balance updates minimal. This will never be a competitive game and does not have to be perfectly balanced, and players shouldn’t have to re-learn the game with monthly or quarterly updates. However, the original 6 warbands should be balanced enough so that they can be used as a measuring stick when developing other warbands.
